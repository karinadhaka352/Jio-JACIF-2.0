# 📊 JACIF 2.0: 45-Item Consumer Empirical Survey Instrument
**Project Focus:** Validating LLM Digital Twins against Indian Consumer Data  
**Target Sample Size:** $n = 300+$ Real Indian Mobile Users  
**Scale Used:** 5-Point Likert Scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree) unless specified.

---

## 👥 Section 1: Demographics & Base Stratification (6 Items)
*These items correspond directly to the 6 target Jio consumer personas.*

1.  **D01 [Age]:** What is your age? (Open-ended numeric input)
2.  **D02 [Gender]:** What is your gender? (Male / Female / Non-binary)
3.  **D03 [Location]:** Which city do you currently reside in? (Used to map Tier-1/2/3 stratification)
4.  **D04 [Income]:** What is your approximate personal/household monthly income?
    * Below ₹15,000
    * ₹15,001 - ₹30,000
    * ₹30,001 - ₹60,000
    * ₹60,001 - ₹1,000,000
    * Above ₹1,000,000
5.  **D05 [Primary Language]:** Which language or linguistic mix do you primarily use when texting or searching online? (English / Hindi / Hinglish mix / Regional Language)
6.  **D06 [Primary Platform]:** Which digital platform do you interact with most frequently for shopping or streaming? (JioCinema / JioMart / Meesho / Amazon.in / Flipkart / MyJio)

## 🎬 Section 2: Conjoint Attributes & Willingness-to-Pay (WTP) (9 Items)
*Used to plot willingness-to-pay curves to detect any synthetic 'wrong-sign' distribution errors.*

### Part A: Streaming Service Tiers (JioCinema Context)
7.  **WTP_01:** I am willing to pay a premium price monthly for an OTT subscription if it guarantees completely ad-free 4K video playback.
8.  **WTP_02:** I prefer a cheaper mobile-only streaming plan even if it forces me to watch 30-second unskippable commercial advertisements during live sports events.
9.  **WTP_03:** Bundling regional language content options (e.g., Hindi, Tamil, Marathi commentary) significantly increases the monetary value of a streaming subscription to me.

### Part B: E-Commerce & Grocery Delivery Options (JioMart/Meesho Context)
10. **WTP_04:** I am willing to pay an extra delivery fee if an e-commerce application guarantees my groceries will arrive in under 15 minutes.
11. **WTP_05:** I purposefully choose platforms that offer deep wholesale discounts or bulk buy-one-get-one deals, even if the delivery window takes 2 to 3 days to arrive.
12. **WTP_06:** Having a hassle-free, no-questions-asked cash-on-delivery (COD) return policy is a requirement for me to purchase clothes online.

### Part C: Telecom Plans & Mobile Features (MyJio Context)
13. **WTP_07:** I am willing to upgrade to a more expensive monthly cellular plan specifically to get unlimited, unrestricted 5G data access.
14. **WTP_08:** I actively look for prepaid recharges that come with free bundled subscriptions to streaming networks like Disney+ Hotstar or Prime Video.
15. **WTP_09:** I prefer to maintain my secondary SIM card active using the absolute cheapest annual validity plan possible, regardless of data limits.

## 📱 Section 3: Technology Acceptance Model (TAM/UTAUT2) (12 Items)
*Measures perceived usefulness, ease of use, and habits within India's digital ecosystem.*

16. **TAM_01 [Perceived Usefulness]:** Using AI-driven search models (like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity) helps me discover product recommendations much faster than standard keyword search engines.
17. **TAM_02 [Perceived Usefulness]:** Interacting with automated customer service chatbots saves me time when resolving issues with my network provider.
18. **TAM_03 [Perceived Usefulness]:** Personalized app recommendations make my digital shopping and content streaming experiences highly efficient.
19. **TAM_04 [Perceived Ease of Use]:** Voice search features in my native language (or Hindi/Hinglish) make it incredibly easy to use apps without typing.
20. **TAM_05 [Perceived Ease of Use]:** Navigating e-commerce chat interfaces to place orders feels clear, direct, and understandable.
21. **TAM_06 [Perceived Ease of Use]:** Completely digital configuration processes, such as online eSIM activations, are simple to execute without a physical store visit.
22. **TAM_07 [Linguistic Comfort]:** I feel more comfortable interacting with digital systems when they utilize localized expressions or mixed conversational phrasing (like Hinglish).
23. **TAM_08 [Linguistic Comfort]:** Pure English interfaces feel distant or overly formal during customer service interactions.
24. **TAM_09 [Linguistic Comfort]:** AI voice assistants that naturally match my regional speech tone build a clearer sense of platform usability.
25. **TAM_10 [Digital Literacy Habit]:** I prefer to handle all product exploration via digital platforms rather than seeking assistance in brick-and-mortar stores.
26. **TAM_11 [Digital Literacy Habit]:** I regularly leverage online peer reviews and social commerce platforms before completing any transactions.
27. **TAM_12 [Digital Literacy Habit]:** I trust algorithmically generated recommendation feeds more than sponsored brand banner advertisements.

## 🔒 Section 4: Consumer Privacy Calculus & DPDP Awareness (9 Items)
*Maps consumer risk-benefit trade-offs under the live DPDP Act 2023 regulatory anchor.*

28. **PRIV_01 [Awareness]:** I am aware that India has implemented the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act to legally protect my information online.
29. **PRIV_02 [Awareness]:** I actively check context notices like "Why am I seeing this recommendation" on digital storefronts.
30. **PRIV_03 [Awareness]:** I understand my rights to request an enterprise to completely delete my data footprint from their operational servers.
31. **PRIV_04 [Calculus]:** I am completely comfortable sharing my live browsing and purchase histories with platforms if it directly results in better product discounts.
32. **PRIV_05 [Calculus]:** I feel uneasy when a retail application tracks my offline location data even if it claims to optimize local delivery speeds.
33. **PRIV_06 [Calculus]:** I view explicit privacy consent popups as helpful trust-building elements rather than annoying interface disruptions.
34. **PRIV_07 [Expectations]:** I expect corporate applications to be completely honest regarding whether a product recommendation is organic or sponsored.
35. **PRIV_08 [Expectations]:** Digital systems should notify me clearly whenever they utilize my historical service parameters to personalize an AI chatbot conversation.
36. **PRIV_09 [Expectations]:** Protecting my online privacy is more important to me than receiving highly personalized digital platform features.

## 🎭 Section 5: AI Emotion & Interface Trust (9 Items)
*Measures trust vs. psychological reactance to adaptive emotional AI mirroring in customer interfaces.*

37. **EMO_01 [Trust Vector]:** When an automated customer assistant expressions empathy (e.g., saying "I understand your frustration"), I feel more valued as a consumer.
38. **EMO_02 [Trust Vector]:** I prefer to execute routine service adjustments using a fast, purely robotic, non-emotional interface.
39. **EMO_03 [Trust Vector]:** An AI that mimics human emotional tone feels more competent and solution-oriented than a rigid command menu.
40. **EMO_04 [Reactance Vector]:** When an automated chat interface acts overly friendly or empathetic, it feels manipulative or artificial.
41. **EMO_05 [Reactance Vector]:** It feels uncomfortable when an AI system behaves as if it possesses genuine human emotional understanding.
42. **EMO_06 [Reactance Vector]:** I experience a distinct sense of intrusive "creepiness" when a chatbot uses my name and buying history to synthesize conversational pleasantries.
43. **EMO_07 [Transparency Interplay]:** Seeing a clear data-usage disclosure note immediately reduces my skepticism toward an empathetic AI interface.
44. **EMO_08 [Transparency Interplay]:** If an AI assistant mirrors my emotions without disclosing that it is analyzing my profiling context, my overall trust in the brand declines.
45. **EMO_09 [Transparency Interplay]:** I am highly likely to continue interacting with an AI interface if it combines data-sharing control parameters with polite, adaptive service behaviors.
